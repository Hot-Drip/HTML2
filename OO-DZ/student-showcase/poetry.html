Poetry

Inside

Inside Moments of shock and horror
spring out of nowhere.

The ordinary turns
to sheer terror
before a breath can be drawn.
What seemed secure
is stripped,
shredded,
shattered.
Then courage rings out—
a bell long cast
in ways unknown
for such a moment as this.
I have marvelled at courage—
not the kind announced
from a platform,
but the kind formed
where no one thinks to look.
One hammer blow
upon the anvil.
Then another. The child thought foolish
for speaking too boldly
has weighed his words
more carefully
than those who laugh.
Then comes the moment.
He opens his mouth,
and truth walks out
before fear can shut the door.
The soldier in the prison line,
or at the front,
perceives the imperceptible:
what is coming.
His intuition knows:
Now is the time.
Before anyone can call him back,
he is already moving.
No trumpet sounds.
No badge appears.
The badge is worn inside.
Yet animals, too,
see and know.
Consider the cat:
a scoundrel with whiskers,
a thief of warm chairs,
naughty by reputation
and innocent of malice.
He watches from the windowsill
as though the whole human enterprise
has been poorly organised.
Then the one he loves is threatened.
Out he flies—
claws, courage, and all—
with no thought for the hurt
that might come to him.
When it is over,
he returns to the windowsill
and washes one paw.
The Committee for Courage
arrives to present zee medal.
Yet watch—
he knocks it under
the festooned sofa.
They crawl hither and thither.
“Where is that—?”
Mr Whiskers watches.
At last, a sparkle.
De paw darts down,
flips the medal
dexterously farther in,
and back to washing
he does go.
“Does this mean he declines?”
the chairman croaks
in a solemn baritone.
“No,” says the child.
“He knows where it is
He’ll play with it again.”

: John-Finn: Kavanagh.
